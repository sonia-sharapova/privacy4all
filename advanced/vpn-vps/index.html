WHO'S WATCHING YOU ONLINE?

Every time you get on the internet, you are not alone. Before you protect yourself, it helps to know who is actually watching:

Wi-Fi Operator

Whoever runs the network you're connected to — a coffee shop, an office, a landlord.

Your ISP

Your Internet Service Provider (Optimum, Verizon, and the like) sees every unencrypted request you make.

Lawful Interception

Government or law enforcement requests, made through legal channels.

Unlawful Interception

Hackers and other bad actors who want your data for their own purposes.

The uncomfortable truth: you are always being watched. An unencrypted connection can leak your IP address, and your IP address can reveal your physical location — so network privacy is about physical safety too, not just data. A quick tell: look at the start of a web address. HTTP versus HTTPS, where the S stands for secure. You want the S.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A VPN connects you to another computer somewhere else in the world through an encrypted tunnel, and you borrow that computer's IP address instead of the one your ISP handed you. It doesn't make your activity disappear — it makes it much harder to link back to you. Does privacy even matter anymore? Yes, and the earlier you start, the better: on the internet, companies and corporations are waiting to consume you.
