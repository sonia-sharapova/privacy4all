FINDING YOUR DATA

Before you can remove your data, you need to know what's out there.

Step 1: Google Yourself

Search for your name, email addresses, and phone numbers:

  • "First Last" (in quotes)
  • "First Last" "City" (with location)
  • "First Last" phone number
  • email@domain.com

Take screenshots of what you find — these are your removal targets.

Step 2: Use People Search Tools

These sites specialize in aggregating your information — check each for a listing:

  • Whitepages.com
  • Spokeo.com
  • BeenVerified.com
  • PeopleFinder.com

The BADBOOL List

The Big Ass Data Broker Opt-Out List (BADBOOL) is a large, free, community-maintained list of data brokers with direct opt-out links. Doing it yourself takes longer than a paid service, but costs nothing. Start with its highest-priority tier of roughly 14 sites, then work down the list over time.

GOOGLE'S "RESULTS ABOUT YOU" TOOL

Google lets you request removal of search results that show your personal contact info (like a phone number or home address) directly from Search — even if the underlying page stays up. Search "results about you" in Google, or visit your Google Account's "Results about you" page to submit removal requests.

REMOVE FROM SPECIFIC SERVICES

AI Services

Most major AI providers (OpenAI, etc.) let you request deletion of your account data and chat history from account privacy settings. Be aware that opting out of a company's model-training program is often a separate toggle from account deletion — check both.

Social Media Accounts

justdelete.me is a directory of direct account-deletion links and difficulty ratings for hundreds of social platforms and web services.

Old Accounts

Check your password manager or old email inbox for "welcome" emails to find accounts you forgot you had, then delete or deactivate the ones you no longer use — a dormant account is still a data source.
